## Implementation facts

- These are live motion mockups, **not** application assets or production renderers. The background canvas is 320×180, displayed at 1920×1080 with nearest-neighbour scaling. The folder and plate remain separately drawn UI layers.
- The animation loop targets roughly 30 paints/second. Only a visible, selected, playing variant paints. Motion pauses offscreen, when the document is hidden, under reduced motion, or via the pause button.
- SNES experiments use original palettes, tile-map sampling, screen-space pixel patterns and sprite-like geometry. Their purpose is console-era visual language, not hardware emulation.
- PS1 experiments share a small software triangle renderer: perspective projection, whole-pixel vertex snapping, affine texture coordinates, a depth buffer, original 16×16 texture patterns, distance fog and 5-bit colour-channel quantization with ordered dither.
- All art, meshes, patterns and cover placeholders are authored in code. No image-generation model or third-party game assets are used in these motion studies.
- The optional host `Tweak` control changes motion speed. The ten-option source stores `consoleExperiments: { shortlist, folder }` through widget state. Navigation itself does not mean selection for implementation, and no messages are sent.

## Constraints and next steps

The original [brief](../console-backdrops-brief.md) describes the Stage, systems, previous attempts and app plumbing. It is historical context, not the latest visual decision. The owner explicitly said **“Rules about steps and size are not important”**. Do not limit a new visual experiment to six updates/second or an arbitrary bitmap budget. Equally, those relaxed design limits are not evidence that a live CPU renderer is suitable for the Thor.

1. Open the latest lab and continue judging the **motion composition**, with and without the folder. Ask for favourites or iterate on a named option. Avoid restarting with illustrated towns, architectural scenes or static generated backgrounds.
2. Develop the chosen effects: tune composition, palette, density, scale, motion amplitude and timing. Keep the folder and case readable. The newest lab has a folder preview only; case/game readability still needs a dedicated pass before integration.
3. Explore the other consoles after this direction is settled. Only SNES and PS1 have accepted-direction motion studies. The earlier all-sixteen ideas document is unapproved historical brainstorming, not a specification to implement.
4. Choose a production rendering approach **after** the effect is chosen. Possible approaches include pre-rendered loops/layers, transform-driven compositions or a suitable accelerated renderer. Preserve the actual motion that the owner likes. Do not assume the earlier “static base plus two water frames” plan still applies.
5. App integration remains separate work. The prior plumbing proposal—per-system `BackdropId`, value equality through `HandheldPlatform`/`HandheldBackdrop`, no restart when browsing games within one system, bounded transitions and lifecycle handling—is still worth evaluating. The current app control is `src/EmuShelf.UI/Views/Handheld/Motion/HandheldEraBackdrop.cs`.
6. Measure the chosen implementation on the Thor for heat, battery, memory, frame pacing and readability. No device acceptance has happened. Keep MVVM and repository constraints, including original/licensed assets and preserving users' game files.

No application source was changed by these studies. ROADMAP H4 is not completed by this design work. Existing rejected studies remain in git as history and are labelled accordingly.

## PS2 and 3DS round (2026-11-07, Claude)

The owner asked for five PS2 and five 3DS options in the same approach, with each one stylistically representing its platform. Open [ps2-3ds.html](ps2-3ds.html) directly in a browser. Use arrow keys or the picker below the preview, and Space to pause. Shortlist and folder visibility are stored in this browser's localStorage only. No option has been chosen yet.

**How each platform is told apart.** Identity comes from the rendering technique and the motion, not from scenery:

- **PS2:** rendered at 480×270 and upscaled with smoothing. A shared software renderer handles perspective-correct Gouraud colour, sub-pixel vertices (none of PS1's snapping or affine wobble), a depth buffer, fog and alpha/additive blending. On output it applies a two-step bloom and a faint alternating-field interlace.
- **3DS:** rendered at 400×225, the 3DS top-screen pixel density cropped to 16:9, and upscaled nearest-neighbour. Shading is banded cel shading with a specular dot and no anti-aliasing. One post pass draws 1px ink outlines at object edges, plus a screen-space drop shadow wherever something sits nearer than what is behind it, which is the "pops out of the screen" cue. Every 3DS camera sways around a fixed pivot plane, so near and far parallax read like stereo depth.

| System | Name / ID | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| PS2 | Glass towers / `ps2-towers` | See-through columns rise and fall over a reflective floor while the camera drifts |
| PS2 | Light trails / `ps2-trails` | Additive particle streams orbit the folder, with frame-feedback trails on deep blue |
| PS2 | Chrome sea / `ps2-sea` | A smooth reflective surface rolls toward a hazy low sun (a smooth, soft counterpart to PS1 Crystal tide) |
| PS2 | Pearl orbs / `ps2-orbs` | Glossy chrome-banded spheres circle the folder on a tilting ring |
| PS2 | Light shafts / `ps2-shafts` | God rays turn behind the folder, with dust motes drifting through them |
| 3DS | Pop-out layers / `3ds-layers` | Scalloped paper-like frames stacked in depth, sliding in parallax with hard shadows |
| 3DS | Toon blocks / `3ds-blocks` | Cel-shaded cubes and pyramids tumble upward, some near enough to pop out |
| 3DS | Bubble depth / `3ds-bubbles` | Toon bubbles stream from deep in the screen toward the viewer |
| 3DS | Depth slider / `3ds-slider` | Concentric frames shaped like the 3DS screen sit flat, spread into a tunnel, then settle back |
| 3DS | Turntable toys / `3ds-toys` | Toon primitives hop around a checkered turntable with blob shadows |

Checked in the desktop app's browser pane: all ten render with no console errors, and stills of each were reviewed with the folder shown. Software render cost in that browser was about 1–10 ms/frame at 30 fps (Glass towers is the heaviest). This is not an on-device measurement. The owner has not reviewed this round yet. The SNES/PS1 lab and its fragment were not changed.

**Owner verdict on this round:** "I love everything ps2 and nothing from 3ds." All five PS2 animations are approved as a set. All five 3DS animations are rejected. Their bright primary colours, cel shading with ink outlines, and drop shadows read as toy-like clipart. Round 3 replaces them.

## Round 3: random rotation, new 3DS and DS (2026-11-07, Claude)

The owner's product idea: **each console folder has five animations, and the backdrop plays them in random order.** [console-rotation.html](console-rotation.html) prototypes this for all five consoles in one file. The SNES/PS1 renderers are copied unchanged from `experiments.fragment.html`, and the PS2 renderers unchanged from `ps2-3ds.html`. In Random rotation, one animation plays for 12 s, then a 1.6 s crossfade moves to a different one picked at random (never the same one twice in a row). The viewer uses two stacked canvases, so rendering doubles briefly during a crossfade. A single animation can also be pinned for review. Console, mode and folder visibility are stored in localStorage only.

The PS2 set was liked because it is atmospheric and cohesive: soft light, one palette, calm motion. The new sets aim for that quality bar while keeping each platform's own rendering signature.

**3DS** renders at 400×225 with no anti-aliasing, but now uses per-pixel "fragment lighting" in the style of the 3DS GPU: stepped diffuse ramp, crisp lookup-table specular, Fresnel rim and bump mapping. The palette is pearl and aqua with sparing coral. A sway around a pivot plane gives the stereo parallax. Nothing is outlined or cel-banded.

| Name / ID | What moves |
|---|---|
| Glass panes / `3ds-panes` | Rounded glass panes drift at several depths and catch a moving light |
| Tile wave / `3ds-tiles` | Glossy pillow tiles pop toward the viewer in a slow radial wave |
| Glossy knot / `3ds-knot` | One large aqua knot turns slowly, threading in front of and behind the folder |
| Kinetic curtain / `3ds-curtain` | Glossy slats ripple in depth and turn into the light |
| Bump relief / `3ds-relief` | Bands of embossed hex domes swell and settle under a circling light. The centre stays calm. |

**DS** renders at 256×144 with 6-bit colour channels. Its identity has two sides. One is the stylus/touch screen: 1px pixel lines on grid or ruled paper. The other is the DS 3D hardware: low-poly flat shading through a toon table, stepped linear fog, and edge marking with per-object outline colours. The palette is polar white, ice blue and navy.

| Name / ID | What moves |
|---|---|
| Stylus loops / `ds-stylus` | Three invisible styluses draw looping strokes around the folder; the ink fades behind them |
| Tap ripples / `ds-ripples` | Taps land around the folder and send out 1px rings |
| Fog lattice / `ds-lattice` | The camera glides through an endless low-poly lattice that dissolves into stepped fog |
| Dot tunnel / `ds-dots` | Two rippling planes of pixel dots stream past above and below |
| Flipbook doodles / `ds-flip` | Hand-drawn doodles boil and morph at 8 fps on ruled paper |

Verified in the desktop app's browser pane: all 25 animations render with no console errors. Stills of every DS and 3DS animation were reviewed with the folder shown and tuned for density. Random rotation advances on its own after 12 s, and Skip crossfades to a different animation. Browser render cost is about 0.5–12 ms per frame per animation. The heaviest are 3DS Tile wave and PS2 Glass towers, and cost doubles during the crossfade. This is not an on-device measurement. The owner has not yet reviewed round 3.

- **25 improved** (new ids ending `-v2` or renamed).
