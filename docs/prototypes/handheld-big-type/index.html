It plays itself until you touch it. Then: arrow keys move, Enter is A, Esc is B, Y opens the menu. Or tap a tile, or the pad beside the bottom screen. When the on-screen keyboard is up you can just type.

EmuShelf · Handheld mode on the Thor · one direction, live

Big Type

Today's Handheld mode puts every name, number and hint inside a small frosted pill. This sketch takes the pills away. The name of whatever you are on fills the top screen, over that console's own backdrop, and nothing changes without moving.

New in round 3. Colour and Round are locked, so the other grounds and typefaces are gone. A on an empty slot names a new folder, and the menu's Search finds games, both with the letters landing on the top screen as you type. Y on a home tile can pick it up and move it. Add to folder now works.

What moves

Fifteen moves, numbered so you can say “keep 1, 3 and 6, drop 8”.

  1. The name is the picture

    The focused folder or game is set as large as the screen allows: a monogram for a console, the full title for a game, wrapped to fit beside its case. No name plate.

  2. Letters arrive from where you came

    Move right and the new name rolls in from the right, letter by letter, while the old one leaves to the left. Open a folder and the letters rise from the hinge.

  3. Colour floods from the tile

    Cocoon's colour wave, across both screens. The console's colour ripples out from the tile you landed on, crosses the hinge, and wipes that console's backdrop in above.

  4. A selector that stretches

    The ring reaches for the next tile before it lets go of the last one, then breathes at rest like the 3DS cursor. The tile under it lifts and its neighbours make room.

  5. Numbers roll

    The clock, game counts, play time and achievements turn like a counter instead of swapping.

  6. Folders deal their games

    Home tiles fall away from the folder you opened, its name lands as a large heading, and the games are dealt in from the corner. Long folders slide a column at a time, with the next column peeking in.

  7. Launch through the hinge

    The cover leaves the bottom screen upwards, the case above catches it, and the console's colour takes the whole top screen with the title on it.

  8. Never quite still

    Leave it alone for a few seconds and a slow hop runs across the grid and through the letters. The case keeps swaying, as it does today.

  9. Squarer, matte tiles

    Corners come down from today's phone-style squircle to something nearer a 3DS icon. No gloss, no gradient, no shadow. The monogram is sized to fill the tile.

  10. Nothing in a pill

    Clock, battery, button hints, sort and counts are plain type set straight on the screen, in the four corners.

  11. One ring for everything

    The same ring that sits on a tile shrinks onto a dock app and widens around a menu row. It never disappears and comes back as a different highlight.

  12. Menus and Settings are type, not cards

    The grid steps back and the rows slide in as plain type on the same colour. The row you are on becomes the big word above, with its value under it. Switches spring and values roll.

  13. While you play, the session counts

    The bottom screen goes dark in the console's colour and shows one large number: how long you have been playing. An unlocked achievement rolls the count and stretches the bar.

  14. Typing fills the top screen

    Name a folder or search, and each letter drops onto the top screen at full size while the word resizes to make room. A deleted letter falls off the end. Search deals matching games in above the keys as you type.

  15. Pick up and put down

    A tile you are moving lifts and wobbles in your hand. It trades places with whatever it passes, across pages too, and B puts everything back where it was.

What it keeps, what it drops

Keeps

  • Every console's backdrop. These are your approved renderers, rotating per console.
  • The 3D case for a focused game, swaying.
  • The 5×4 home grid, empty slots, 4×3 folders that scroll sideways, and the dock.
  • Monograms on console folders.
  • The same Settings sections and menu actions as today.

Drops

  • Frosted pills and the name plate.
  • Gloss, gradients and shadows on tiles.
  • White cards for menu rows and Settings rows.
  • The big folder icon repeated on the top screen.
  • Indigo as the only colour: each console brings its own.

The system so far

Sizes are for the Thor's screens (1920×1080 over 1240×1080). The mock draws at half of that.

Type

Face
M PLUS Rounded 1c, weights 500, 700 and 900. It is on Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License; shipping it needs a line in the third-party notices.
Display
Weight 900, line height 0.9. Not a fixed size: the name is fitted to a 1360×680 box (1120 wide beside a case), up to three lines.
Heading
100. Folder name, Settings, the session timer at 380.
Label
44 bold. Console name, play time, counts.
Row
40 bold, value at 32. Menus and Settings.
Hint
38 bold beside a 58 outlined button glyph.
Monogram
Fitted to 76% of the tile's width.

Shape

Tile
176 square on home, 208 in a folder, corner radius 30. Flat fill.
Ring
8 stroke, 10 clear of the tile, radius 50 on a tile and 32 on a row.
Focus
Tile grows to 110%, neighbours move 8 away.

Motion

Land
cubic-bezier(.2, 1.5, .35, 1), 380 to 540 ms. Anything arriving: letters, tiles, digits, the ring.
Leave
cubic-bezier(.5, 0, .9, .5), 170 to 250 ms. Leaving is always quicker than landing.
Stagger
12 ms per digit, up to 26 ms per letter (300 ms in total at most), 45 ms per tile along the diagonal.
Ring
300 ms. Covers both tiles at 38% of the way, then lets go.
Flood
640 ms circle from the tile. The top screen follows 110 ms later, 760 ms, from the matching point on the hinge.
Scroll
500 ms with a slight overshoot, one folder column or one home page.
Idle
A hop after 4.8 s without input, then every 6.5 s.

Colour

Bottom screen
The focused console's colour, flat, edge to edge. Tiles are white at 17%; the focused one is solid white with the colour as its ink.
Per console
One flat colour each, dark enough for white type.
Top screen ink
White, or the console colour mixed 74% toward near-black, chosen per corner from the backdrop behind it.
In game
The console colour mixed 64% toward near-black.

Decided, and still open

  • Decided: the bottom screen takes the console's colour, and the type is Round.
  • Open: which of the fifteen moves stay, and anything that is too much.
  • Open: whether five blue-to-violet consoles (PS2, PSP, DS, GBA, GameCube) are far enough apart now that colour is the identity. I left the palette alone.

Covers are drawn stand-ins with the title as text. The backdrops are the approved prototypes from the motion study. X (swap screens) is not drawn yet. Menu rows other than Play, Open, Add to folder, Move on home, Search, Settings and Sounds only close the menu. A folder you make lasts until the page reloads. Nothing here has been measured on the Thor.
